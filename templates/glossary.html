Scoring Indicators

Score 0 – 100
The app's overall buy-signal strength for a stock right now. It is a weighted average of four technical indicators (RSI, MA Crossover, Momentum, Relative Volume), each converted to a 0–100 sub-score and combined. Higher = stronger signal. It is not a price prediction — use it as a first-pass filter, then do your own research.
RSI Relative Strength Index
A number from 0 to 100 that measures how fast a stock has been moving recently compared to its own history.
  • Below 30 — often considered oversold: the stock may have fallen too far, too fast, and could bounce.
  • Above 70 — often considered overbought: the stock may have risen too far, too fast.
  • 40–60 — neutral zone.
This app scores RSI highest when it is below 40 (potential buying opportunity) and flags it as a risk when it is above 70.
MA Crossover Moving Average Gap
Compares the stock's 20-day simple moving average (SMA) to its 50-day SMA and shows the percentage gap between them.
  • Positive % — the short-term average is above the medium-term average, meaning recent price action is trending up.
  • Negative % — recent trend is below the medium-term, a bearish sign.
A 20-day crossing above the 50-day is sometimes called a Golden Cross — a classic bullish signal.
20d Momentum
The percentage price change over the last 20 trading days (roughly one calendar month). Positive momentum means the stock has been climbing; negative means it has been falling. This app uses it as a trend-confirmation signal — rising stocks tend to keep rising in the short term, and vice versa.
Rel Vol Relative Volume
Today's trading volume divided by the 20-day average daily volume.
  • 1.0× — normal activity.
  • 1.5× or higher — elevated — more traders than usual are buying or selling, which can amplify price moves.
  • Below 0.5× — quiet day; signals may be less reliable.
High relative volume often accompanies breakouts or news events.
{# ---- Risk flags ---- #}

Risk Flags

Low Liquidity
The stock trades less than $1 million of shares per day on average. Low-liquidity stocks are harder to buy and sell without moving the price yourself, and bid-ask spreads tend to be wider — meaning you pay more to enter and get less when you exit.
High Share Price
The current share price is above $50. With a $100 account you can only buy one whole share (or less) before using half your capital. This flag is a reminder to check whether your broker supports fractional shares (Robinhood does).
Overbought
The RSI is above 70, meaning the stock has risen sharply in a short time and may be "due for a pullback." It does not mean it will fall — strong stocks can stay overbought for a long time — but it does mean you are buying at a point of elevated momentum, not at a discount.
High Volatility
The stock's 20-day annualized standard deviation of daily returns puts it in the top 25% of the watchlist. Higher volatility means larger daily price swings — which amplifies both gains and losses. With a small account, a single bad day can wipe out a meaningful percentage of your position.
Earnings Soon
The company is scheduled to report quarterly earnings within the next 14 days. Earnings reports often cause large, unpredictable price gaps — the stock can jump 10% up or down overnight. Buying just before earnings is closer to a coin-flip than a technical trade.
{# ---- Market cap ---- #}

Market Cap & Size

Market Cap Market Capitalization
The total market value of a company's outstanding shares: share price × total shares. It tells you the company's overall size as the market currently values it — not the same as revenue or profit.
Cap Size Categories
  • Micro-cap — under $300 million. High risk, high potential volatility, often less analyst coverage.
  • Small-cap — $300 million to $2 billion. More growth potential than large caps, but less stable.
  • Mid-cap — $2 billion to $10 billion. Often a balance of growth and stability.
  • Large-cap — $10 billion to $200 billion. Established companies, generally lower volatility.
  • Mega-cap — over $200 billion. Think Apple, Microsoft, Amazon. Very liquid, very stable, slower growth.
Use the Market Cap slider on the dashboard to focus on a particular size tier.
{# ---- Position sizing ---- #}

Position Sizing & Portfolio

Position Sizing
How much of your account to put into one stock. This app caps any single position at 20% of your account (configurable in config.py). With a $100 account that means a maximum of $20 per stock — limiting the damage if a pick goes wrong.

The stock detail page shows exactly how many shares $20 would buy at the current price.
Paper Trade
A simulated trade where no real money moves. You record a "buy" at today's price, then track how it would have performed over time. It is a way to test whether the app's signals actually lead to good outcomes without risking real money.
P&L Profit & Loss
The dollar gain or loss on a position.
  • Unrealized P&L — the gain/loss on a still-open position, using the current live price. It becomes real only when you sell.
  • Realized P&L — locked-in gain/loss after you close a position.

Price & Chart Terms

SMA Simple Moving Average
The average closing price over the last N trading days, updated daily as new prices come in. The chart on each stock's detail page shows the 20-day SMA (short-term trend) and 50-day SMA (medium-term trend). When the shorter line is above the longer line, the stock is in a short-term uptrend.
Delayed / End-of-Day Data
All prices in this app come from Yahoo Finance's free tier, which provides end-of-day closing prices — not live quotes. The price shown is the last official closing price, not the current intraday price. Always check your broker for the live price before you place an order.
Rank Delta (↑ / ↓)
The small badge next to a ticker on the dashboard (e.g. ↑3, ↓2) shows how many positions that stock moved in the ranking since the last time you clicked Refresh Data. It compares the current score-based rank to the most recent saved snapshot.
  • ↑3 — moved up 3 spots (improved signal).
  • ↓2 — fell 2 spots (weakening signal).
  • new — not present in the previous snapshot (newly added to watchlist or first refresh).
